## STEP 1. Unanchored thinking

### (a) The problem in one sentence

**The person who wrote the rules is not the person shopping, and at the moment of buying the rules get bent without her: a product is "close enough", or its page never states the number at all.**

Shorter, for a slide: *Rules are written by one person and bent by another at the checkout. An agent bends them faster and nobody sees it.*

Why this one and not "accountable": every judge today has watched an agent quietly relax a constraint to finish its task. They will nod at "the agent substituted" before they nod at "accountability".

### (b) Three openings, about 20 seconds each

1. **Whose rules.** "Every shopping agent takes criteria. The question nobody asks is whose criteria they are, and who is allowed to bend them. When no product fits, the agent bends them, or the shopper does. Here neither can. Only the person who wrote the order can change it, and nothing reaches a cart until she has signed exactly what will be bought and why."
2. **What an approval is worth.** "When an agent shops for you, 'approve' means a total and a thumbnail. That tells the approver nothing she cares about. Does the seat reach the height I wrote? Does the store's page even state it? This is an approval where she sees the store's own words next to her limit, and her signature covers those words."
3. **The failure.** "A therapist writes: seat height seventeen to nineteen inches. The daughter's agent finds a good one, well reviewed, cheap. The page never states the seat height. A shopping agent buys it. Ours sets it aside, says why, and when nothing meets the rule it does not guess. It asks the one person who is allowed to change the rule."

### (c) Who really has a reason to approve

Ranked by how true and how common the approval is today.

| # | Approver | Exists today? | How painful now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Manager or finance approving spend against a policy | Yes, everywhere (requisitions, purchase approvals, expense policy). | The approver sees a total and a description and clicks. Policy checks happen after the money is spent. Most common, least vivid. |
| 2 | Grant or school budget holder | Yes. Purchases must fit the award's or the district's allowed-cost rules, and someone other than the buyer signs. | Slow, paper, audited afterwards. Buyers pay out of pocket rather than wait. |
| 3 | Payer authorising medical equipment | Yes, and strict: covered equipment is paid only against a clinician's order, some items need prior authorisation. | Very painful, but it runs through enrolled suppliers and claims systems, not a family's cart. Much home safety equipment is not covered at all, so the family buys it retail from a paper list. |
| 4 | Contractor who guarantees what he installs | Informally. He specifies, the owner buys, he refuses to warrant the wrong part. | Common and costly (returns, a second visit), no tooling. |
| 5 | Clinician (therapist) | The recommendation exists: a written equipment list at discharge. The approval of a purchase does **not** exist. The family buys alone. | The pain is real but it is the family's: wrong height, wrong width, returns, a call to the clinic. |
| 6 | Parent | Yes (ask-to-buy on phones and consoles). | Low stakes; approves an item and a price. |

Reading of the table: the therapist is the most **human** example and the weakest as an **existing approval**. Spend approval is the most real and the least moving. So: tell the therapist story, and say out loud that the same sheet is what a manager, a budget holder or a payer would sign.

Facts checked: see "Facts" at the end.

### 1. Verdict on the framing

**"The accountable person is not the shopper" is close, and one word is wrong.** "Accountable for the purchase" is not true of a therapist: she answers for her recommendation, not for what the family buys. A judge who knows healthcare will catch it, and it is the root of the builder's own doubt. Say **"the person who wrote the rules"**. That is true of Dana (the order on screen says "The therapist sets these limits. The person shopping cannot change them."), and it is true of a manager, a budget holder and a payer without changing a word.

**The sharper problem is the bending, not the approving.** Every agent demo today takes criteria. What they do when nothing fits, or when the page does not state the number, is relax and carry on. Sign-Off's claim is that neither the agent nor the shopper may relax a rule; only its author can, and her answer is signed. That is the answer to "shopping agent with criteria and nothing more": *whose criteria, and who is allowed to bend them.*

**Keep "all they get to approve is a total", cut "every purchase".** "With agents that becomes true of every purchase" is the best idea in the integrator's line (once an agent shops for you, you are the person who was not there) and "every" is an overclaim. Move the idea to the close, as "once agents shop, we are all the person who was not there".

**Is the therapist the right approver to lead with?** For the story, yes: one named person, a date, a house, and a wrong purchase anyone can picture. As an approval that exists today, no, and the pitch should say so before a judge does. Lead with the general sentence, tell Rosa's story, and name the approvers who already approve (a manager, a grant holder, a payer) in the close. Do not lead with an insurer: nothing built touches a payer, and most of this very list is not something Original Medicare pays for (see Facts), so "a record the family can show an insurer" invites a question that cannot be answered well. Cut that reason.

**What is weak, plainly.**
- No therapist approves a family's cart today. She writes a list at discharge and the family buys alone.
- On the lead data every item has a passing product, so "when no product meets her rule, only she can change it" is **not what the screen shows**. The Ask Dana beat is Elena wanting a different limit, not a dead end. Say that.
- Nobody on the other side checks the signature. A store takes the cart with or without it.
- Unless the builder has talked to a working therapist, "she would do this" is a guess. Say "I have not tested this with a therapist" if that is the case.

**"Would she really approve a cart?" The best true answer:**
> "Not a cart, and not today. Today she writes a list and the family guesses in the aisle. What she does here takes half a minute: she answers the one question only she can answer, whether her own rule can change, and she confirms that what will be bought is what she ordered, in the stores' own words. I have not put this in front of a working therapist yet. The approvers with the habit already are the ones who sign for other people's spending: a manager, a grant holder, a payer."

## Facts

Checked with one web search (15:17), no page opened; treat as lightly verified.
- Medicare Part B covers medically necessary equipment such as walkers and commode chairs when a provider orders it for home use, through a Medicare-enrolled supplier: [medicare.gov, commode chairs](https://medicare.gov/coverage/commode-chairs), [Humana, durable medical equipment](https://www.humana.com/medicare/durable-medical-equipment).
- Grab bars and raised toilet seats are generally not covered by Original Medicare (treated as convenience or self-help items); some Medicare Advantage plans give an allowance for such items: [AARP](https://www.aarp.org/medicare/faq/does-medicare-cover-home-safety-equipment/), [CMS coverage determination, DME reference list](https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/details/ncd-details.aspx?NCDId=190).
- **Unverified:** whether shower chairs are covered (the search found no clear statement); what prior authorisation involves for any of these items; that therapists never review a family's purchases (my knowledge of ordinary practice, not a source); every line of the approver table in step 1 other than the two Medicare points.

Consequence for the pitch: the walker on this sheet is the kind of item a payer pays for against an order, and the grab bar is the kind a family buys alone. That is a good answer in questions ("this is the part of the list nobody checks today") and too fine a point for the stage.
